Worked example: Why can ₹1,00,000 still show 0 lots?

Assumptions: Capital ₹1,00,000 · Entry premium ₹100 · Stop-loss premium ₹80 · Lot size 65 · Capital buffer 5% · Risk budget 1% · Brokerage ₹20 per order · Other charges estimate 0.10%.

1. Buying budget: ₹1,00,000 − 5% buffer (₹5,000) = ₹95,000 available for the position.

2. Entry cost for one lot: ₹100 × 65 = ₹6,500 premium value; add estimated entry brokerage and other charges (about ₹26.50) = about ₹6,526.50. Capital alone can cover multiple lots.

3. Risk budget: ₹1,00,000 × 1% = ₹1,000 maximum planned risk for this trade.

4. Estimated risk for one lot: (₹100 − ₹80) × 65 = ₹1,300 planned premium loss, plus about ₹51.70 estimated round-trip charges = about ₹1,351.70.

Result: 0 lots. One lot fits the capital budget, but its estimated stop-loss risk (₹1,351.70) is greater than the ₹1,000 risk budget. The calculator uses the stricter limit.

To explore the result, change the risk percentage or stop-loss distance only if it matches your trading plan. A wider risk budget permits more risk; it does not make the trade safer. Actual charges and stop-loss execution can differ.
